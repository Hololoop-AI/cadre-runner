Goal & current state

Goal: the pipeline works end-to-end; quality is now the constraint. Build the machinery to measure stage and pipeline quality (deterministic checks + LLM judges), climb a graduated test ladder from tiny fixtures to live projects, and close the loop with a meta-agent that reacts to eval failures by proposing improved prompts — the first reflective skill-refinement tier of Cadre itself.

Current state: evals/ has fixture stories with deterministic expected-claims, scoring.py, and Wilson-interval stats. It cannot judge artifact quality (is the spec briefing grounded? is the risk grade justified? does the demonstration demonstrate?), has no calibration labels, no observability backend, and no improvement loop. Prompts live as files in the repo with no versioning or provenance.

2 · Judges

Per-criterion binary PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN, isolated call per criterion group, and a verbatim quote required as evidence — every criterion is extraction against a supplied reference (story, diff, test output), never “is this good?”. That is the defense against generator and judge both being Claude: reference-anchored extraction drops judge false-positive rates from 0.72 to ~0.01 in the published measurement, and cross-tier judging (Sonnet judging Opus output) breaks self-recognition. Judged claims become a second claim kind in the existing expected.json schema — scoring and stats interfaces unchanged.

JudgeWhy this order
1Spec briefing groundedness & sufficiency — 7 criteria Upstream of everything; structure checks can never see whether the briefing describes the story it was given. Cheapest to calibrate — you already read every one at gate 1.
2Risk grade justification — 6 diff-anchored criteria The only judgment with direct merge authority. Includes a criterion killing unverifiable self-praise and one catching “low” beside an unaddressed migration. Risk-triage itself gets no judge — scored as precision/recall against your adjudications.
3Demonstration faithfulness — 6 criteria Where your attention concentrates at gate 2; overstatement is the fastest trust-eroder. Every claimed behavior must tie to a passing test or shown artifact; every done-criterion addressed or reported unmet.

3 · Calibration — ~30 minutes of your time, total

Manufactured negatives

~20 seeded corruptions of accepted artifacts (delete a story constraint, invent a file reference, downgrade a risk grade with a migration in the diff). Gives a real true-negative rate — a working pipeline produces mostly passes, so a judge that always says PASS would otherwise look excellent — and measures self-preference directly.

The one ask

One sitting: 15 shuffled artifacts, pass/fail + a one-sentence critique each. Critiques become the rubric language and few-shot examples. ~32 ground-truth items total — under-powered by the literature's standard, and reported as such.

Metrics & upkeep

Per criterion: Cohen's κ (headline — raw agreement overstates judges by 30–40 points), TPR, TNR, UNKNOWN rate; ≥3 runs at temp 0. Kill any criterion with κ < 0.4. Every judge/driver disagreement at a gate auto-joins the calibration set. After any optimizer round: re-validate the judge on fresh labels — a practice documented nowhere in the field, and the self-play result (judge pass rate 0.72→0.94 while true accuracy stayed 0.20) is why.

Fixture stories written SWE-bench-Pro style: a requirements list stating expected behavior, not implementation (OpenAI retired SWE-bench Verified after finding 59% of audited problems defective — this is their fix). Grading files never enter the agent's workspace: with a visible scorer, frontier models exploited label information in 403 logged runs, and stronger models exploit more. Stats: single-run deltas of 2–3 points are noise (it's the inference stack, not us) — hence n=8 at the cheap rungs, and paired McNemar's for variant comparisons (~10× cheaper than independent arms; small addition to stats.py).

diff + scores + rationale] H -- driver approves --> G H -- driver rejects --> I[candidate archived,
lineage kept] G --> J[next runs use new version
provenance recorded] J --> A

Registry, not PRs (your call, and consistent with your standing state-not-in-VC position): prompts move out of the codebase into a runner-owned registry — content-addressed versions with lineage (parent, producer: hand edit / reflection / optimizer, eval scores, model pinning), plus a git-backed export for portability: hand a prompt to a teammate or use it outside the system. Every stage run records its prompt version, so A/B provenance is free. Long-term this registry is the skill-registry vision — entries with attached eval evidence are what make skills addable, shareable, and eventually community-publishable; the same machinery pointed at an empty slot is skill creation; a workflow entry is a manifest of stage-prompt versions.

Adoption gate (all must hold; “no adoption” is the normal outcome): beats the seed on a frozen fixture split under paired McNemar · survives false-discovery correction across candidates · no judge TPR/TNR degradation on fresh labels · survives one end-to-end run · diff contains no fixture-specific nouns · hard fail on any deterministic-claim regression or any seeded-corruption pass · length penalty in the metric (measured exploit: 14.7× length inflation scoring higher).

Risks & open questions

Repo no longer self-contained. Prompts-in-registry means a bare checkout doesn't reproduce behavior; the git-backed export mitigates but doesn't erase this. Eyes-open cost of the choice.

Judge-hacking is the standing threat, not a tail risk. Measured: optimized prompts flagged as prompt-hacking 86% of the time while scoring higher; strict rubrics (ours) overfit harder than lenient ones. The adoption gate + post-round judge re-validation are the mitigations; the promotion surface keeps you in the loop at human cadence until trust is earned.

Expect a small accuracy prize. Where seed prompts are already strong, published gains are mostly latency/length, not correctness. Phase 0 exists to find out before we spend.
